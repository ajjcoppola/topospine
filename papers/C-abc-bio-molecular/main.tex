\begin{abstract}
We extend the certificate-carrying \textsf{TopoSpine} pass manager from
complexes to circuits and point clouds.  (i)~\emph{Covering-aware collapse}
replaces a column of a covering IR by an admissible free face \emph{in place},
preserving column count and weights and recording a decode map, so that the IR
can never be silently emptied (the failure mode of deletion-style collapse).
(ii)~A small And--Inverter-Graph / factor-diagram IR, with a
\emph{don't-care spine} pass that solves the covering problem whose rows are only
the care ON-set and whose columns are primes of $\mathrm{ON}\cup\mathrm{DC}$, then
factors the result; every pass is re-checked by an independent evaluator and
exact optimality witnesses are produced for min-cube covers.
(iii)~A \emph{relative SpineTower} for molecular conformation clouds, forcing an
$\varepsilon$-net of a designated subset $A$ (functional group) at every scale
and reporting exact $\mathbb F_2$ Betti numbers of $N$, $N_A$ and $(N,N_A)$.
All benchmarks here are on \emph{synthetic} bio circuits and a \emph{synthetic}
conformation cloud; no claim is made about real pathways or real molecules.
Against a real Berkeley~ABC baseline (run through \texttt{yosys-abc}), the don't-care
spine beats ABC applied without don't-cares on every synthetic cascade, and ABC applied
to our output is better still; ABC cannot use our don't-cares in this flow.  A same-budget
ablation of the relative tower shows a smaller covering radius on the designated subset
than a $k$-center baseline of equal size, but \emph{no demonstrated topological advantage}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Covering-aware collapse}
Let $T_C$ be the graded covering table of a cubical complex: columns are top
cells, rows are all faces, incidence is the face relation.  For a top cell $c$
with free face $f$ (no other top cell contains $f$), the 2007 collapse-branch
replaces the column of $c$ by that of $f$ and recurses; an optimal cover of the
reduced table decodes to one of the original table under the hypothesis
\textsf{CoverAdmissibleFreeFace} (every row incident to $f$ is covered only by
columns containing $f$).

\begin{proposition}[IR-level invariants]\label{prop:ir}
Let $\mathrm{replace}_{c\to f}$ substitute $f$ for $c$ in a list of column ids.
Then $|\mathrm{replace}_{c\to f}(X)|=|X|$; if $X\neq\emptyset$ then
$\mathrm{replace}_{c\to f}(X)\neq\emptyset$; and if $f\notin X$,
$\mathrm{decode}_{f\to c}\circ \mathrm{replace}_{c\to f}=\mathrm{id}$.
\end{proposition}
These are proved in Lean~4 (\texttt{CoveringCollapse.lean}; the proofs use only
\texttt{propext}).  Optimality transport itself is the abstract
\texttt{transport\_optimum} (L5); its four cost/feasibility hypotheses are
\emph{not} proved for covers and remain the admissibility hypothesis, checked
only by the Python auditor.

The implementation (\textsf{CoveringAwareCollapsePass}) is fail-closed: it
refuses cells in the designated relative subcomplex, refuses inadmissible
faces, and its certificate is rejected by the independent checker if the column
count changes, the inserted face is absent, the dimension does not drop, or the
decode map is missing.  \emph{Negative result.}  On the 2007 cyclic-core complex
$\{{--00},{-0-0},\dots\}$ \emph{every} free face is inadmissible
(6 of its 9 columns have free faces, 12 in all), so the pass inserts nothing; on disjoint squares it
reduces each square to a vertex in two steps while keeping both columns.

\section{AIG, factor diagrams, and the don't-care spine}
A single-output incompletely specified function is a triple
$(n,\mathrm{ON},\mathrm{DC})$.  \textsf{DontCareSpinePass} builds the table with
rows $=\mathrm{ON}$ (don't-cares are never rows), columns $=$ prime implicants of
$\mathrm{ON}\cup\mathrm{DC}$, and solves it with \emph{plain} branch-and-bound (the
collapse-branch is only run as an audited comparator, because it is suboptimal on some
$n=5$ instances; see Paper~A).  Whenever $\le 16$ columns remain an exhaustive oracle
audit is attached and the result is labelled \textsf{ProvenMinimum} for the chosen
objective: \texttt{cubes} (default), \texttt{literals}, or \texttt{cubes\_then\_literals}
(minimum over covers of ON by primes of $\mathrm{ON}\cup\mathrm{DC}$).  Single-output
\texttt{.pla} and combinational \texttt{.blif} inputs ($\le 10$ inputs) are read by
\texttt{topospine.formats}.  \textsf{FactorDiagramRewritePass} applies algebraic quick-factoring
into a structurally hashed AIG and is rejected if it would increase the AND
count.  The checker \texttt{verify\_logic\_trace} evaluates the realized AIG on
every minterm and rejects any ON minterm mapped to $0$ or OFF minterm mapped to
$1$.  A \textsf{CoverOptimalityWitness} (decoded cover, cost) is verified by the
Loop~1 checker.  An adapter \textsf{AbcBaselineAdapterPass} writes BLIF and runs
\texttt{strash} plus an explicit expansion of \texttt{resyn2} through Berkeley~ABC
(\texttt{yosys-abc}, installed for this study); it never mutates the state, and
ABC's AND counts are baseline numbers only (we do not re-verify ABC's output).

\section{Synthetic bio circuits}
\emph{Gene-regulation toy.}  Four next-state functions of a 5-gene toy network
(not a published model) with satisfiability don't-cares from two stated
invariants ($\mathrm{CycE}\Rightarrow\mathrm{E2F}$,
$\mathrm{CycA}\Rightarrow\mathrm{CycE}$).
\emph{Pathway cascade.}  A seeded feed-forward cascade of 24 or 40 four-input nodes over 6
primary inputs.  The local don't-cares are the \emph{exact} unreachable fan-in
patterns over all $2^6$ primary-input assignments; hence replacing each node by
any function agreeing with it on the reachable patterns preserves every node
value on every primary input, which we verify exhaustively (0 mismatches).
Table~\ref{tab:bio} compares an unoptimized minterm SOP, the same pipeline
without don't-cares, and the don't-care spine; gate counts are summed over nodes
without cross-node sharing.

\input{../../benchmarks/results/bio_benchmark_table.tex}

On every node of every benchmark circuit the don't-care spine used no more gates
than the no-DC pipeline (an empirical observation, not a theorem: the factoring
step is heuristic), and every node carries a verified witness.  ABC with
don't-cares forced to $0$ is better than our own no-don't-care pipeline but worse than the
don't-care spine in every cascade row (e.g.\ 298 vs.\ 142 ANDs on the 40-node, seed~1
cascade); running ABC on the spine's output gives a further small gain (137).  On the ISCAS
\texttt{c17} netlist, per-output synthesis uses 8 gates versus 6 in the shared original:
we do no cross-output sharing.  Changing the cover objective from cubes to literals reduces the total
literals of 120 random 5-variable incompletely specified functions only from 2830 to 2824
(cubes unchanged at 776; $\approx5\%$ of functions differ).  We stress that the cascade structure and its
don't-care density are produced by our generator; the percentages should not be
read as typical of biological or industrial circuits.

\section{Relative SpineTower on molecular conformations}
Let $P$ be a point cloud (atoms pooled over conformations) and $A\subseteq P$ a
designated subset (functional group).  At scale $s$ we take pieces
$B(c,s)$; the \emph{relative tower} forces centres at an $s/2$-net of $A$, then
covers the remaining points by a greedy irredundant spine over columns passing
the collapsibility oracle.  Let $N$ be the nerve of the selected pieces
(2-skeleton), $N_A$ the subcomplex of faces with a common point in $A$, and
$(N,N_A)$ the relative pair; Betti numbers are exact over $\mathbb F_2$ (checked
against Euler additivity on every level).  Certificates: \textsf{RelativeEquivalence}
(each $A$-point lies in a piece centred in $A$) and \textsf{ApproximatePersistence}
(selected refinement maps; \emph{no} interleaving proof).

\input{../../benchmarks/results/molecular_benchmark_table.tex}

On the synthetic 156-point cloud (13 atoms $\times$ 12 torsion/jitter conformations,
$|A|=48$) the relative tower resolves $A$ at radius $\approx 0.6,0.27,0.14$ versus
$2.33,1.17,0.60$ for a coverage-only spine, at the price of $15,31,43$ rather
than $4,13,19$ pieces.  This is a trade-off, and the same-budget ablation of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} tempers it.  A $k$-center baseline with the same number of
pieces reaches $r_A=1.11,0.38,0.27$ (relative tower: $0.60,0.27,0.14$), so forcing an
$A$-net is roughly a $1.4$--$2\times$ improvement in $A$-resolution; but the relative tower
is \emph{worse} on the global radius ($1.61$ vs.\ $1.11$ at the coarsest scale) and the
exact $\mathbb F_2$ Betti numbers of the $A$-nerve agree with an own-code Vietoris--Rips
reference on $A$ at twice the scale at the same levels as for the $k$-center baseline (two of three; the coverage-only spine matches at one of three).  We therefore
claim \emph{no topological benefit}, only improved $A$-resolution at equal budget.
MD17/MD22 is supported through a local \texttt{.npz} (\texttt{TOPOSPINE\_MD17\_NPZ}) but was
not run; GUDHI was not installed.

\section{Lean and claim status}
Lean: Proposition~\ref{prop:ir} (list-level covering-collapse invariants); a concrete
\texttt{decide}-checked triangle contraction (L3) and an $\mathbb F_2$ cup-product Leibniz
rule (L6) are described in Paper~B.  Schema-level only: the cover specialization of
transport, which is the admissibility hypothesis checked only by the Python auditor.
\textbf{Mode caveat.}  In the covering mode of the pass manager the pass is vacuous on the 2007
core and on a solid $8^3$ voxel blob (no admissible free face), so Section~1 is a
correctness and negative-result statement, not a performance result.
